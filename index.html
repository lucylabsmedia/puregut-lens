What the evidence says, and its limits

ADDapt trial. King's College London randomised 154 adults with mildly to moderately active Crohn's disease for 8 weeks. Symptoms responded in 49% on the low-emulsifier diet and 31% on the control diet. When this guide was written, the results were available only as conference abstracts.

Which additives were tested. ADDapt's control group ate snacks containing carrageenan, carboxymethylcellulose and polysorbate 80. Every other entry here is flagged from the CDED avoid list, from lab studies, or by chemical similarity. Each card states its basis.

Evidence against. A 4-week Australian feeding trial found no difference in Crohn's disease activity between high- and low-emulsifier versions of a healthy diet.

CDED. The Crohn's Disease Exclusion Diet (Levine and colleagues) pairs whole foods with partial enteral nutrition over three phases. It restricts whole food groups as well as additives, so a clear additive screen does not make a product CDED compliant.

Sources. ADDapt abstract, Australian feeding trial, CDED systematic review. Product data comes from Open Food Facts under the Open Database License.
